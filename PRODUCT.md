# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Music listeners who connect their Last.fm, Spotify, or Google Sheets scrobble history and get personal music charts — top Songs, Artists, and Albums over customizable time periods. Ranges from casual users checking their monthly favorites to music-obsessive "data nerds" who track detailed listening stats and enjoy badges/streaks. Used on both desktop and mobile, often to screenshot and share results.

## Product Purpose

Turn raw listening/scrobble data into readable, personal, shareable charts. Core loop: connect a data source (Last.fm, Google Sheets, or CSV upload) → view ranked charts per section (Songs/Artists/Albums) → customize size/period/theme → share a chart as an image. Success looks like a user checking back regularly and sharing their charts the way people share Spotify Wrapped.

## Brand Personality

Data-nerdy & playful. Precise, information-dense stat presentation (rankings, play counts, badges) combined with fun, game-like touches — BU badges, multiple expressive color themes, share-as-image moments. Not a dry corporate analytics dashboard.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS admin-dashboard aesthetics (flat gray cards, boring data tables, corporate blue). Reference point instead: Spotify Wrapped / Receiptify-style stats apps — bold, shareable, stat-card driven, personality-forward.

## Design Principles

- Charts are the hero — rankings, cover art, and numbers get visual priority over chrome/controls.
- Shareable moments matter — anything a user might screenshot (chart views, share-as-image output) should look intentionally good, not just functional.
- Respect the data nerd — don't dumb down or hide precise stats in the name of simplicity; make density readable, not remove it.
- Personality through theme, not clutter — the multiple color themes (gold, pink, violet, teal, etc.) carry the "playful" identity, so structural UI can stay clean and get out of the way.
- All three data sources are first-class — Last.fm, Google Sheets, and CSV uploads must work equally well in any feature, never designed Last.fm-first.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard best-effort: reasonable contrast, adequate touch targets on mobile, `prefers-reduced-motion` support. No formal WCAG AA audit requirement for this pass.
