Setup Guide

Three ways to get your listening history in. Pick the one you started with and follow it end to end.

Back to charts →

What you'll need

  • A Google account
  • A Last.fm account with some listening history
  • A free Last.fm API key — don't have one? Step 2 creates it, takes two minutes

  1. Copy the template sheet

    Open the template and click Make a copy. It lands in your own Drive — private to you.

    Open the template →

  2. Get your free Last.fm API key

    The sheet pulls your plays through Last.fm's API, and that needs a key of your own. Free, instant, no approval — sign in to Last.fm and fill in the form at last.fm/api/account/create. The values can be anything:

    FieldWhat to enter
    Application nameMy Music Charts
    DescriptionPersonal use
    Homepage (optional)https://dankcharts.fm
    Callback URL (optional)https://dankcharts.fm

    Copy the API Key it shows you — that's all this path needs. It also shows a Shared Secret; ignore that here, it's only for scrobble pushing.

  3. Fill in your details

    In your copy, open the Settings tab at the bottom and fill in column B.

    Column AColumn B — fill these in
    Last.fm Username: your_lastfm_username
    Last.fm API Key: your_32_character_api_key
    Data Tab Name: leave as-is

    The API key is the one you copied in Step 2 — paste it exactly, no extra spaces.

  4. The script is already there

    It came with the template — nothing to add. To see it:

    Extensions → Apps Script

    Close that tab and carry on.

    Replace the script manually

    Paste this into Extensions → Apps Script, replacing everything there. Save with Ctrl+S, then redeploy if you set up a Web App in Step 7.

    Approve it — one time only
    
            
    The first time you run anything from that menu, Google asks you to approve the script. It walks you through four screens, and one of them looks scarier than it is:
    
            
    
              
    Screen you'll seeWhat to click

              !
              
    
                "Unsafe" reads worse than it is. Google shows that screen for every personal script that hasn't gone through its commercial review — including this one, which is your copy sitting in your Drive. It fetches your plays from Last.fm and writes them to this one sheet. Nothing else, and nobody else can run it. Every line is readable under Extensions → Apps Script.
              
    
            
    

            
    What happens after that
    
            
    Nothing lands instantly — the first batch of plays arrives about a minute later, then a fresh one every 90 minutes. Each sync resumes from the last play it saved, so nothing is fetched twice.
    
            
    It runs on Google's servers, so you can close the sheet and the browser entirely. Want the newest plays right now instead of waiting? Last.fm → Fetch Latest Tracks runs the same sync on the spot.
    
            
    
              Why 90 minutes?
              
    
                
    Some services — Deezer especially — lag before their scrobbles reach Last.fm. A wider window means nothing gets missed.

    Connect it to dankcharts.fm
    
          
    
            
    Your sheet is filling up with plays — now point the site at it. This is the last step you actually need.
    

            
    First, let the site read the sheet
    
            
    dankcharts.fm reads your sheet straight from your browser and never signs in to your Google account, so a private sheet gives it nothing back. Open the Share button at the top right of your sheet and change one setting:

                This doesn't publish your listening history. "Anyone with the link" means the link is the key — the sheet isn't listed in anyone else's Drive, and nobody stumbles across it. Viewer means even someone holding the link can only read it, never change it.
              
    
            
    

            
    Copy the link from that same dialog
    
            
    While you're still in the Share dialog, use its Copy link button — not the URL in your browser's address bar.
    
            
    
              !
              
    
                The address bar carries a hidden tab pointer. It ends in #gid=…, naming whichever tab you happen to be looking at — and that quietly overrules the tab name you set below. Copy it while the Settings tab is open and the site reads your Settings tab, finds no plays, and shows you empty charts with no error. Copy link gives you a clean URL with no pointer.

    Leave Tab name empty unless you renamed the data tab in your copy — empty means Full Raw Listening History, which is what the template calls it.
    
            
    Hit Save & Load. Your plays are read on the spot and your charts appear. That's the setup done — Step 7 is optional, and only worth it if you want to change your listening history from the site rather than in the sheet.
    
          
    
        
    
      
    7. 

      
  7. 
        
        
    
          
    Let the site write back to your sheet Optional
    
          
    
            
    Everything so far is one-way: the site reads your sheet. Skip this step and your charts work perfectly — you just can't change anything from the site. Spend five more minutes here and three things switch on:
    
            
      
              
    • + Add Play — log a play that never scrobbled (a record, a live set, a friend's aux cord)
      • 
              
    • Fixing a play sticks — the ✎ button in Raw Data writes the correction into the sheet itself, instead of only tidying what you see in this browser
      • 
              
    • Autocorrect rules save — "this misspelling always means that artist" gets remembered and applied to the sheet on every load
      • 
            
    

            
    Deploy the script as a Web App
    
            
    The script that's been syncing your plays can also listen for messages from the site — but only once it's published with a URL of its own.

              →

              i
              
    
                The type isn't a dropdown. In that dialog, click the small gear icon beside "Select type" and choose Web app from it. It's the step most people hunt around for.

              !
              
    
                "Anyone" is wider than it sounds, and it's the only setting that works. Your browser calls this script without being signed in to Google, so it has to accept an anonymous request. Execute as: Me keeps it running with your permissions on your sheet and nothing else. The URL is what stands in for a password — it's long and random, but treat it that way: anyone you hand it to could add rows to your sheet.
              
    
            
    
            
    Press Deploy. Google asks you to authorise it, exactly as in Step 5 — same Advanced → Go to … (unsafe) → Allow path.
    

            
    Copy the URL it gives you
    
            
    The deployment finishes on a Web app URL — a long one ending in /exec, like https://script.google.com/macros/s/AKfy…/exec. Copy it, then paste it into the site. It lives one fold down from where you put your sheet link:

              →

              →

Troubleshooting

      

        

          The Last.fm menu doesn't appear in my sheet.
          

            
Close and reopen the sheet. The onOpen function runs when the sheet loads — if you had the sheet open right after making your copy, just refresh the page.

          

        

        

          No data is being imported.
          

            
Check that your Last.fm username (B1) and API key (B2) in the Settings tab are correct and have no extra spaces. You can test by clicking Last.fm → Fetch Latest Tracks and then going to View → Logs in the Apps Script editor.

          

        

        

          The site shows "No Google Sheet configured".
          

            
Make sure you've pasted the sheet URL in dankcharts.fm Settings and that the sheet is shared as "Anyone with the link can view".

          

        

        

          My charts are empty, but the sheet is full of plays.
          

            
Look at the URL you pasted into Settings. If it ends in #gid= followed by a number, it's pointing at one specific tab — whichever one was open when you copied it from the address bar. When that's the Settings tab, the site reads three rows of configuration instead of your listening history and shows empty charts without complaining.

            
Fix it by deleting everything from #gid= onwards, or by re-copying the link with the Copy link button inside the sheet's Share dialog, which never includes one.

          

        

        

          Manual entry gives an error.
          

            
The Web App URL must be redeployed after any script change. In Apps Script: Deploy → Manage deployments → edit the existing deployment → Deploy.

Troubleshooting

      

        

          The "Push to Last.fm" button doesn't appear in the edit panel.
          

            
You need to complete Step 3 first. Open Settings → Last.fm → Scrobble Setup and check that the status shows "Connected".

          

        

        

          Clicking "Connect to Last.fm" gives an error.
          

            
Make sure both your API Key and Shared Secret are filled in under "Use custom API credentials". The button won't work with just a username.

          

        

        

          "Push to Last.fm" shows an error after connecting.
          

            
Your session may have expired. Go to Settings → Disconnect, then reconnect following Step 3 again.

          

        

        

          The scrobble appears twice on Last.fm.
          

            
That's expected — the push added the corrected version but the original is still there. Delete the original manually on the Last.fm website (Profile → Scrobbles).

What you'll need

    
    
      
  • A data export from your music service — or any spreadsheet of plays
    • 
      
  • A spreadsheet editor, if your export needs reshaping (Google Sheets, Excel and Numbers all work)
    • 
      
  • Patience: most services take a few days to send your data
    • 
    


    

      ✦
      

        Your file never leaves your device. It's read and parsed in your browser, then stored locally — nothing is uploaded to a server.
      

    


    

      

        

        
        
      

      

        
        
      

    


    
    

      
  1. 
        
        
    
          
    Get your export
    
          
    
            
    Where's your history coming from?
    
            
            
    
              
              
              
              
              
              
            
    

            
      
              
    • 
                
      Spotify.zip · drop it in
      
                
      Account → Privacy settings → Download your data. Tick Extended streaming history — "Account data" is only the last year.
      
                
      Arrives by email as a ZIP. Upload it without unpacking.
      
                
      
                  How long does it take?
                  
      
                    
      A few days for account data, up to 30 for extended history.
      
                  
      
                
      
              
      • 
              
    • 
                
      Deezer.xlsx · one edit first
      
                
      Settings → My personal data → request your data.
      
                
      You get a workbook of several sheets, and only the first one is read. Drag your listening history to the front, save, upload.
      
              
      • 
              
    • 
                
      Last.fm.csv · drop it in
      
                
      No built-in export — use ghan.nl's exporter: username → scrobbles → CSV. No API key, no sign-in, no editing afterwards.
      
                
      
                  Two things worth knowing
                  
      
                    
      Over 100,000 scrobbles takes a minute or two to build.
      
                    
      It can fetch only what's newer than a previous export — but uploads here replace rather than merge, so keep the full file.
      
                  
      
                
      
              
      • 
              
    • 
                
      YouTube Musicconvert first
      
                
      Google Takeout → deselect everything → YouTube and YouTube Music → limit to history.
      
                
      Takeout's ZIP isn't a shape we read. Turn its watch-history file into a CSV — Step 3.
      
              
      • 
              
    • 
                
      Apple Musicrename headers
      
                
      privacy.apple.com → Request a copy of your data → Apple Media Services information.
      
                
      The play-history CSV uses Apple's own column names — rename the header row in Step 3.
      
              
      • 
              
    • 
                
      Something elserename headers
      
                
      Tidal, Amazon Music, SoundCloud, Plex, a DJ set log, a spreadsheet you keep by hand — anything with a track, an artist and a date works. Step 3 has the column names.
      
              
      • 
            
    

            
    
              Menu doesn't match what you see?
              
    
                
    These paths move around. Search your service's help centre for "download my data" — the export itself is what matters, not the route to it.
    
              
    
            
    
          
    
        
    
      
    2. 

      
  2. 
        
        
    
          
    Can it go straight in?
    
          
    
            
    Three shapes need no editing. If yours is one, skip to Step 4.
    
            
    
              
    FileWhat's read
    
              
    .zipSpotify streaming history
    
              
    .xlsx / .xlsfirst sheet only
    
              
    .csvcolumn names from Step 3
    
            
    

            
    
              
    Spotify ZIP
    
              
    Upload it whole. Plays under 30 seconds are dropped, and so is anything without both a track and an artist — which is how podcasts fall out. A lower total than you expected is usually that, not a failure.
    
            
    

            
    
              
    Deezer XLSX
    
              
    Only the first sheet is read, so move your listening history to the front. English or French headings both work — titre, artiste, album, écoute.
    
            
    

            
    
              
    A CSV you already have
    
              
    If its header row already uses a name from Step 3, it's ready. ghan.nl exports are — their extra MusicBrainz ID columns are simply ignored.
    
            
    
          
    
        
    
      
    3. 

      
  3. 
        
        
    
          
    Make it a CSV
    
          
    
            
    
              ✦
              
    
                You can skip this step. Your export already imports as it comes — head to Step 4.
              
    
            
    
            
    The universal path: any export becomes importable once row 1 uses these names.
    
            
    
              
    ColumnHeader — any one of these
    
              
    
                Song title (required)
                song title · title · track · track name · song name
              
    
              
    
                Artist (required)
                artist · artist name · performer
              
    
              
    
                Date and time (required)
                date and time · date · datetime · timestamp · time · played at · scrobble time · uts · utc_time
              
    
              
    
                Album (optional)
                album · album name · release
              
    
            
    
            
    One row per play — a track you've heard forty times is forty rows.
    
            
    
              !
              
    
                Format dates as YYYY-MM-DD. 03/04/2024 is read as March 4th, not April 3rd — a day-first export lands a whole year on the wrong days.
              
    
            
    
            
    
              What else is accepted?
              
    
                
    Extra columns are ignored, capitalisation doesn't matter, and column order is irrelevant. Renaming row 1 is the whole job.
    
                
    Dates work as 2024-03-09 21:14:05, 2024-03-09, ISO 8601, 03/09/2024 or unix seconds — as long as the year is 2000 or later.
    
                
    In Google Sheets, reformat a date column with Format → Number → Custom date and time → yyyy-mm-dd hh:mm:ss.
    
              
    
            
    
            
    Save it out with File → Download → Comma-separated values (Excel: Save As → CSV UTF-8). Let the spreadsheet write the file and commas inside song titles look after themselves.
    
          
    
        
    
      
    4. 

      
  4. 
        
        
    
          
    Upload it
    
          
    
            
    
              Settings ⚙
              →
              Upload File
            
    
            
    Drop the file on the box or click to browse — .csv, .zip, .xlsx, .xls. You'll see ✓ N plays loaded and your charts appear.
    
            
    
              i
              
    
                Uploading replaces, it doesn't merge. Combining two services? Join them into one CSV first.

Troubleshooting

      

        

          It says the required columns are missing.
          

            
The header row needs a title column, an artist column and a date column, spelled as one of the accepted names in Step 3. Check row 1 specifically — if your export has a title banner or a blank line above the headers, delete those rows so the header row is the very first line of the file.

          

        

        

          It says no valid records were found.
          

            
Almost always the date column. Every row needs a date that parses to the year 2000 or later — rows without one are skipped, and if that's every row you get this message. Reformat the column to yyyy-mm-dd hh:mm:ss and try again.

          

        

        

          My Spotify ZIP imported far fewer plays than I expected.
          

            
That's usually correct rather than broken. Plays shorter than 30 seconds are skipped, and podcasts and videos are dropped because they have no artist and track pair. If you got the basic "Account data" export rather than Extended streaming history, it also only covers about the past year.

          

        

        

          My Deezer XLSX imported nothing.
          

            
Only the first sheet of a workbook is read, and Deezer's export usually opens on a different one. Move the listening-history sheet to the front of the workbook, save, and upload again.

          

        

        

          My Google Takeout ZIP does nothing.
          

            
Only Spotify's ZIP layout is recognised — Takeout's is different, so nothing inside it is found. Open the watch-history file from the Takeout archive, reshape it into a CSV as described in Step 3, and upload that instead.

          

        

        

          My ghan.nl export won't upload.
          

            
That tool offers XML, CSV and JSON — only the CSV option can be uploaded here. It also exports loved tracks and events, which aren't play histories; make sure scrobbles is the selected export type.

          

        

        

          Everything imported, but the dates are all wrong.
          

            
Your export is almost certainly day-first (DD/MM/YYYY), which is read as month-first. Reformat that column to yyyy-mm-dd hh:mm:ss in your spreadsheet, export again, and re-upload.
